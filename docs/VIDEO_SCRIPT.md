- Speaker B: a second teammate, ideally from a medical or life-science background. Only someone already on the registered team can appear; the team can't be changed after registration.

| # | Time | Screen | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–1:30 | Deck: cover | A | Team, college, one-line promise: test the dose on your twin before you inject it. In-silico proof of concept, not a medical device. Mention the condition: insulin-treated diabetes, validated on type 1. |
| 2 | 1:30–4:00 | Deck: problem, gap | B | The burden in India: about 9.4 lakh people with T1D, about 3 lakh under 20 (IDF 2024). Pens, not pumps; every dose is manual. Night-time hypoglycaemia. Affordable sensors alarm only after the low starts (Abbott: Libre 2 Plus has no predictive alarms). Walk through the calculator table row by row. |
| 3 | 4:00–5:00 | Deck: solution | A | The three jobs: check the night at bedtime, warn before daytime lows (adverse event), test every meal dose (personal treatment). The two data streams the brief asks for: synthetic EHR + wearables (CGM, pen, meals, steps, heart rate, sleep). |
| 4 | 5:00–7:30 | Deck: architecture, model; repo `dosetwin/ehr.py`, `dosetwin/fusion.py`, `twin/model.py` | A | Data fusion in plain words: the record gives weight, prescriptions, HbA1c and eGFR, which become the twin's starting physiology, and the severe-hypo diagnosis, which makes it more cautious. CGM, pen, meals, steps, heart rate and sleep personalise it (MAP fit, about 1.5 s) and keep it synchronised. Show one synthetic EHR JSON (diagnoses, labs, genetics). Keep code on screen under a minute. |
| 5 | 7:30–11:30 | **Dashboard: clinician view** | B drives, A explains | Patients ranked by risk, including tonight's bedtime check. Open the top patient (adult, 10.4% below 70, "Low likely tonight"). Read the metrics against consensus targets and the glucose profile. Read tonight's night forecast: the pessimistic path, the alert line and the suggested snack. Point to the record (diagnoses, eGFR, HLA) and the wearables summary. Settings review: twin estimate vs current vs suggested; explain the 20% guardrail and the low-confidence flag. Click "Try suggestion", read the replay, drag the basal slider. Open a patient with eGFR below 60 and one with a severe-hypo history and say how the twin treats each. |
| 6 | 11:30–14:30 | **Dashboard: dose check** | A, B | Child tab, dinner 60 g at 20:58. Calculator 3.5 U vs twin 1.5 U. Drag the dose up and watch the safety floor turn red. Reveal what really happened: 3.5 U gave 60 min below 70; 1.5 U gave none but peaked at 242 mg/dL. Say plainly that this is a trade, and the twin is a model, not an oracle. Then the adult tab (175 g dinner). Show "What this patient's twin learned", including how much a 4-hour night weakens insulin. |
| 7 | 14:30–15:30 | Deck: design | A | How we avoided fooling ourselves: hidden-truth simulator, different model family, settings and the eGFR coefficient frozen on a development cohort, 3 held-out cohorts, paired arms, refitted ablations. |
| 8 | 15:30–19:00 | Deck: results, bedtime, ehr, alerts, forecast | A, B | Vs affordable threshold-alarm CGM: −77% time below 70, −83% night-time below 70, +4.6 TIR (p < 0.001). Vs premium: better on every measure, none significant. Bedtime check: AUROC 0.86 vs 0.80 (LightGBM) vs 0.53 (bedtime glucose); flags 84% of night lows, median 2.5 h before they start; acting on it cut night-time lows by 27%. Health record: the same twin without HbA1c, eGFR and hypo history spends 1.17% vs 0.93% below 70 (p = 0.01), and 1.26% vs 0.44% in patients with a severe-hypo history. Alerts: 78% caught at ≤ 2 false alerts a day, 26 min warning. Forecasts: best near lows (19.4 vs 23.6 mg/dL). |
| 9 | 19:00–20:30 | Deck: limits | B | Virtual type 1 patients only, cohort enriched for kidney disease and impaired awareness. No 4–7 hour warning: median 2.5 h. Heart rate and sleep gave no measurable benefit. Gains vs premium CGM not significant. Twin settings not better than the clinic's on average, hence the guardrails. What a clinical pilot would need. |
