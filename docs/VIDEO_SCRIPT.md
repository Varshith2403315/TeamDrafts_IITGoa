# DoseTwin demo video: script and shot list (target 22–23 min; the rules require at least 20)

Record with OBS (or PowerPoint's "Record slide show" if you present from the PDF). 1080p, one mic, every tab pre-opened. Upload as an **unlisted** YouTube video and paste the link into the README.
- Speaker A: technical lead.
- Speaker B: a second teammate, ideally from a medical or life-science background. Only someone already on the registered team can appear; the team can't be changed after registration.

| # | Time | Screen | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–1:30 | Deck: cover | A | Team, college, one-line promise: test the dose on your twin before you inject it. In-silico proof of concept, not a medical device. Mention the condition: insulin-treated diabetes, validated on type 1. |
| 2 | 1:30–4:00 | Deck: problem, gap | B | The burden in India: about 9.4 lakh people with T1D, about 3 lakh under 20 (IDF 2024). Pens, not pumps; every dose is manual. Night-time hypoglycaemia. Affordable sensors alarm only after the low starts (Abbott: Libre 2 Plus has no predictive alarms). Walk through the calculator table row by row. |
| 3 | 4:00–5:00 | Deck: solution | A | The two jobs (predict the adverse event; personalise treatment) and the two data streams the brief asks for: synthetic EHR + wearables. |
| 4 | 5:00–7:30 | Deck: architecture, model; repo `dosetwin/ehr.py`, `twin/model.py` | A | Data fusion in plain words: the record gives weight, insulin prescriptions and HbA1c, which become the twin's starting physiology; the CGM, pen, meals and steps personalise it (MAP fit, about 1.3 s) and keep it synchronised. Then the dose advisor and the hybrid alert. Show the synthetic EHR JSON for one patient (diagnoses, labs, genetics). Keep code on screen under a minute. |
| 5 | 7:30–11:30 | **Dashboard: clinician view** | B drives, A explains | The doctor's workflow. Patients ranked by low-glucose risk; open the top one (child, 10.6% below 70). Read the metrics against consensus targets, then the glucose profile by time of day. Point to the record (diagnoses, labs, HLA genotype). Settings review: twin estimate vs current vs suggested; explain the 20% guardrail and the low-confidence flag. Click "Try suggestion" and read the replay: predicted time below 70 on day 7 falls from 6.9% to 4.2%. Drag the basal slider to show the virtual patient responding. Click "Approve changes" and read the note aloud. Open a second patient with a low-confidence flag and explain why the twin keeps the setting. |
| 6 | 11:30–14:30 | **Dashboard: mealtime view** | A, B | Adult tab, breakfast 115 g. Calculator 12 U vs twin 10.5 U. Drag to 14 U and watch the safety floor turn red. Reveal what really happened: 12 U gave 50 min below 70; 10.5 U gave none. Say plainly that the twin overestimated the peak; its dose ranking was right. Child tab: clinic carb ratio 25.9 g/U, twin 54.1, truth 58.6 ("this child was over-insulinised"). Scroll through the one-day comparison chart. |
| 7 | 14:30–16:00 | Deck: design | A | How we avoided fooling ourselves: hidden-truth simulator, different model family, settings frozen on a development cohort, 3 held-out cohorts, paired arms. The dev gain over premium CGM (+5.5) shrank to +2.2 on held-out data; we report +2.2. |
| 8 | 16:00–19:00 | Deck: results, alerts, forecast | A, B | −69% time below 70, −72% night lows, +4.6 TIR vs affordable threshold-alarm CGM (p < 0.001). Vs premium predictive CGM: equal on lows, +2.2 TIR, not significant. Alerts: 80% of lows caught at ≤ 2 false alerts a day vs 71% (trend) and 73% (LightGBM); median warning 26 min. Forecasts: best near lows (19.9 vs 23.3 mg/dL); LightGBM better at 2 h. B explains why false-alarm burden matters (alarm fatigue). |
| 9 | 19:00–20:30 | Deck: limits | B | Virtual patients only. Small dosing gain. HbA1c made even the unpersonalised twin fairly safe; personalisation mainly cuts alerts (4.2 → 2.6 a day). Some raw twin settings were absurd, hence the guardrails. What a clinical pilot would need. |
| 10 | 20:30–21:30 | Terminal + GitHub | A | `python -m pytest -q` (10 pass), `results/study.json`, the README sections, the MIT licence, and one-command reproduction. |
| 11 | 21:30–22:30 | Deck: impact, stack, close | A | Premium protection on an affordable sensor. Roadmap: real-data validation, then insulin-treated T2D, device connectors, ABDM/FHIR, a clinic pilot. End on the promise line. |
