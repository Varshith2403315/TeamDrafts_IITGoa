Why the bolus calculator gets it wrong

What the calculator assumesWhat actually happens
Carbs are counted correctlyCounts are off by 20–50%, with a personal bias
Clinic ratios are rightCarb ratio and correction factor drift; reviews are months apart
Every day is the sameExercise, short nights, the dawn rise and kidney function change the need
Insulin on board is linearAbsorption differs by person, dose and timing

A twin that knows this person's physiology can answer the question the calculator cannot: what will this dose do over the next five hours?

The standard calculator is a fixed formula: carbs divided by ratio plus correction minus insulin on board. It ignores the person-specific dynamics that the twin learns.
